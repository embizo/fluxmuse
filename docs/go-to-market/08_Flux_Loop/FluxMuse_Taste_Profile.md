## Part B: customer taste

**B1. Who buys** (target customers; none are paying yet)

- **Main customer: the WhatsApp side-hustle and micro-business owner.**
  - Braiders and hair studios, home bakers, fashion and sneaker resellers,
    nail techs, caterers, small informal retailers.
  - Mostly in Gauteng townships and suburbs to start.
  - They already sell through WhatsApp Status, DMs and Facebook, run
    everything from one phone, and have no marketing budget.
  - Plans: **Nano R149** and **Micro R289**.
- **Second customer: the small business with staff.** Shops, restaurants,
  salons, clinics, local e-commerce. They juggle a few tools and answer
  WhatsApp by hand. Plans: **Starter R499**, **Growth R1,999**.
- **Third customer: digital agencies and freelancers** managing many small
  clients. **Agency R9,999**, partner price **R6,999**.
- **Who we're not for, yet:**
  - businesses that need paid checkout outside South Africa;
  - anyone wanting Instagram DMs or comments automated;
  - big enterprises wanting a proven vendor. They can talk to us about Custom,
    but we don't chase them.

**B2. How they talk**
- **Day to day:** they speak South African English mixed with isiZulu,
  Sesotho or Setswana, and it's WhatsApp-native: short messages, voice notes,
  emojis.
- **Words and phrases they use** (typical of the segment; replace these with
  real chats once there are customers):
  - "DM to order"
  - "inbox me"
  - "available in all sizes"
  - "eft only"
  - "deposit secures your slot"
  - "sharp"
  - "ayt"
  - "eish, my phone"
  - "month-end special"
- **Tone they respond to:** warm and direct, like a sharp friend who also runs
  a business. Not a bank, and not a Silicon Valley startup.

**B3. What earns trust, and what makes them hesitate**
- **Trust signals:**
  - a clear rand price;
  - seeing it working on their own products (the live demo shop built from
    their public photos);
  - a real person, Thabo, who sets them up by hand;
  - a verified Meta Tech Provider;
  - Free plan with no card.
- **Common worries, and our honest answers:**
  - **"Is this a scam / another AI thing?"** A named founder, a verified Meta
    Tech Provider, and a demo built on their own products before they pay
    anything.
  - **"Will it work in my language?"** The assistant answers in the language
    the customer writes in, and voice notes are turned into text.
  - **"I can't afford a marketing agency."** Nano is R149 a month, about the
    price of a few data bundles.
  - **"I don't have time to learn software."** Most of it works from
    WhatsApp: send product photos and reply YES.
  - **"What if it doesn't bring customers?"** The lead guarantee: 3 qualified
    leads in 30 days. Use it only with its conditions (§ Proof).

**B4. Where they are and when**
- **Channels, most important first:**
  1. WhatsApp (Status and chats)
  2. Facebook (groups and Pages)
  3. TikTok
  4. Instagram
  5. Community word of mouth
- **Best times to reach them:**
  - evenings, 19:00–21:30;
  - Sunday afternoons;
  - around month-end and payday, from the 25th to the 1st.

## Part C: brand taste (the four stories)

- **C1. Brand story:** Every African business deserves a marketing team and a
  checkout, inside the chat app its customers already use. FluxMuse exists
  because the people doing the hardest selling, from one phone, after hours,
  have the fewest tools.
- **C2. Product story:** An AI marketing team on WhatsApp.
  - **Products:** send product photos, and it builds your catalogue and a shop
    link.
  - **Posts:** say "POST", and it writes the caption and makes the picture,
    ready for your Status.
  - **Customers:** the assistant answers customers in their language, day and
    night, and you get a WhatsApp alert for every order.
- **C3. Why us:**
  - Built for African small businesses from day one: WhatsApp-first, priced
    in rands, multilingual, from R149.
  - Run by a real person who sets you up by hand, not a faceless app.
- **C4. Why now:**
  - Founding Member pricing for South African sign-ups: 30% off the first two
    monthly bills, while it lasts.
  - Customers already shop in WhatsApp, and every unanswered DM is a lost
    sale.

## Part E: voice rules for the AI team

**E1. Languages:**
- Plain South African English by default.
- Reply in the customer's language when they write in another one.
- Light, natural code-switching is fine for greetings ("Sawubona", "Dumela",
  "Sharp").
- Ads in isiZulu, Sesotho or another language must be checked by a speaker
  before they go live.

**E2. Always**
- Talk like a sharp friend who runs a business: warm, direct, short sentences.
- Use the customer's name when you have it.
- Lead with a real moment from their day: a missed DM, a Saturday lost to
  admin, a quiet month-end.
- Give the real rand price. Make the next step a WhatsApp message.
- Say "newly launched" or "being switched on" truthfully. Never say "proven".

**E3. Never**
- Say "free trial", "free month", "try it free", "risk-free", "money-back" or
  "refund", or offer any free period other than the Free plan itself.
- Claim customers, ratings, testimonials, results, "trusted by", "pilot
  brands" or any uplift number.
- Promise guaranteed sales, ROI or "10x". The lead guarantee may be mentioned
  only with its conditions.
- Present Instagram or Facebook auto-posting, Instagram DMs, or checkout
  outside South Africa as available.
- Name SnapScan, Stitch, Flutterwave, PayFast or M-Pesa direct.
- Use jargon, hype, AI-for-its-own-sake, a call-centre tone, or the words
  "charity" or "struggling".

**E4. Off-limits:**
- comments on a prospect's finances, health, family or legal matters;
- targeting named individuals;
- unsolicited bulk messages (POPIA s69).

**E5. Visual style:**
- Real African small-business settings, natural light, faces and hands at
  work.
- Flux Orange `#FF6A00` as an accent.
- Ink `#20242B` or Night text on orange, never white on orange.
- Poppins for headlines, Inter for body text.
- No stock-photo office scenes.
