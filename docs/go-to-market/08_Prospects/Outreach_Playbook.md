# Outreach playbook: the first 20 prospects

Prepared 2026-09-26. Read with [CURRENT_OFFER.md](CURRENT_OFFER.md), [Prospect_List.xlsx](Prospect_List.xlsx) and
[Prospect_Briefs.docx](Prospect_Briefs.docx). The proposals are in [proposals/](proposals/).

## The answer in two sentences

Send the wave-1 messages (owner-operators who decide alone) over the next two weeks, but **only after you have run the
Concierge end to end on a real phone and settled the guarantee**, because a pay-first offer with no proof converts only if
the prospect can see it working. Hold Khuli Chana and the other creator-founders until one real merchant is using the product,
because you can approach each of them **once**.

**Confidence:** about 70% on the sequencing. **Expect about one paying merchant from these 20 cold, pay-first approaches, not
20.** On the assumptions in the Pipeline maths sheet (25% reply, 60% take a call, 30% buy) that is roughly 3% end to end, so
you would need **around 85 approaches to land 3 customers.** Demos on the prospect's own catalogue and warm introductions
should convert better, but treat every rate as a guess until you have your own. The list is a way to learn which trades
convert, not a revenue plan.

## What great looks like

In six weeks: **at least 1 paying merchant using the Concierge every week, a consented case study, and a clear read on which
trade converts.** Not "20 proposals sent". Three paying merchants in six weeks needs a bigger list or warmer routes than these
20, so plan a second list of 40 to 60 prospects, or lean on introductions and inbound.

## The options I weighed

| | Approach | Impact | Effort | Risk | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Send all 20 now (the literal ask) | Fast learning | Low | Burns one-shot targets (creator-founders, agencies) before you have proof | No |
| **B** | **Sequenced waves (recommended)** | Learning plus proof, then the hard targets | Medium | Slower start | Yes |
| C | Warm introductions only | Highest close rate | High | Doesn't scale; depends on your network | Yes |
| D | **Flip the ask:** build each wave-1 business a demo shop link from their *public* photos and show it on the call | Best conversion for a pay-first offer | Medium | Needs your approval that a demo is not a "trial" | Yes |

**The option you probably wouldn't think of is D.** A live demo of their own catalogue converts far better than slides, and it
isn't a free period (no access, no usage, nothing to cancel). It fits your pay-first rule, but it is adjacent to it, so
**approve it explicitly** before anyone does it.

## The fatal flaw in my own recommendation

Pay-first, no customers, no results, and a product no real merchant has yet run end to end: **a cold owner has no reason to pay
R289 up front.** The guarantee helps, but it isn't defined yet. If after about 10 calls you have replies and zero buyers,
**the offer is the problem, not the list.** The fix is a founder-approved exception for the first three businesses (for
example, a named-account arrangement in exchange for a case study), or a paid set-up at Nano. That is your call; I haven't
put it in any proposal.

## Cadence (a person sends every message)

- **Day 0:** the opening message in the brief.
- **Day 4:** one short follow-up that adds one useful thing (a relevant post), with an opt-out.
- **Day 10:** a polite last note. Then **stop. Three touches maximum.**
- Log date, channel, message, reply and opt-outs. Record any "stop" the same day.

Day 4: "Hi [name], one quick follow-up on my note about [specific thing]. In case it's useful, here is a short piece on how
[trade] businesses handle [pain]: [link]. If it's not relevant, tell me and I'll leave you be."

Day 10: "Last note from me, [name]. I'll leave it here so I don't clutter your inbox. If handling [pain] ever becomes a headache,
I'm at [contact]. All the best with [specific thing about their business]."

## The 20-minute call

| Minutes | What |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | Who we are, and say plainly that we're new and have no customer numbers yet |
| 3-10 | Their current flow: test the hypotheses in the brief; write down their words |
| 10-15 | Demo on their own catalogue (built beforehand from public photos), if you approve option D |
| 15-18 | Plan, price, payment first, guarantee |
| 18-20 | A clear next step: yes, no, or a dated "not now" |

Don't pitch features they didn't ask about. Never claim results.

## A candidate lead definition (your decision)

You guarantee **leads, not sales**, and only against something measurable and auditable. A proposal:

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| A qualified lead | A **unique WhatsApp number** that starts a conversation with the merchant's connected number **through a FluxMuse-tracked entry point** and sends at least one message |
| Entry points that count | Shop-link click-to-chat, a FluxMuse short link or QR code, or a click-to-WhatsApp ad referral |
| Excluded | The merchant's own staff and test numbers, and repeat numbers within 30 days |
| Evidence | `shop_events` and `lead_events` rows, with timestamp and source |
| The merchant's part | Share the link and post at least N times a week. **Without this the guarantee is unfair to you.** |

The number of leads to guarantee (`N`) is undecided, and I haven't put one in any proposal.

## Measures and kill criteria

- **After the first 10 messages:** reply rate under 10% means the opening is wrong; replies but no calls means the ask is wrong; calls but no buyers after about 8 means the offer or the proof is wrong.
- **At 30 days:** fewer than 2 paying, pause outreach and fix the product or offer. More than 3, double down on the trade that converted.

## A tension to decide, not hide

The shop link sends orders to the merchant's own WhatsApp (off-platform by design). Your policy wants sales to flow through
FluxMuse checkout, which is built but untested with real money. For the first cohort I'd **prioritise activation over
oversight** and switch checkout on once a real payment has been tested. That is your call.
